Habitat Hotel — Chunk 2 Implementation Brief (Milestone M2: "Hands")

Audience: a fresh Claude Code session with no prior context, working in the existing Habitat Hotel GitHub repo (Chunk 1 / M1 is already built and merged). This document is self-contained — read it fully before writing any code, then read the repo (Step 0) so you conform to what M1 actually shipped.

Engine: Godot 4.x, GDScript only (no C#). The exact version is pinned in PINNED_VERSION.md at repo root — use that binary, do not upgrade or change it. All code targets the Godot 4 API — never Godot 3 (await not yield; @onready, @export; CharacterBody2D; Callable-based signals). If unsure whether an API is 4.x, check the installed version's docs, not memory.

Open design decision carried into this brief (flag for review, do not silently change): Training uses a hybrid model — staff gain skill passively by working a station, and the player may optionally put a staffer into a directed Training state to raise one skill faster. Training costs no money (the economy is still M3); the only cost is that a training staffer is off the floor. If the design owner wants a different training model, that's a design conversation — build the hybrid as specified here and note it in DECISIONS.md.

Part A — What You Are Building and Why

Habitat Hotel is a cozy, funny hotel-management sim about running a hotel for animals, for mobile (portrait), premium one-off purchase. The core verb is matching arriving guests to rooms by hand under light, never-stressful time pressure. M1 shipped that core loop: a four-phase day, morning checkouts → dirty rooms → an abstract housekeeping service cleaning them, an afternoon arrival queue with patience moods, and drag-to-seat with gold/amber matching, party splits, pop-ins, save/load, and a night summary.

Chunk 2 = Milestone M2: "Hands" — staff and the cleaning gamble. M1's automatic housekeeping becomes a staff you allocate by hand. You are adding four named staff members, station assignment, a skill + training system, rush-cleaning, bellhop-driven check-in/checkout timing, staff traits, and the screens to see and move your people. The empty-station dilemma — if you pull someone off reception to rush a clean, reception is now empty — is the new toy.

The playtest question this chunk must answer:

"Does pulling a staffer off their station mid-queue to rush a clean feel like a delicious risk — not an annoying chore?"

Everything you build serves that question. The M1 playtest question ("is seating guests fun for ten days?") must stay answered — do not regress the matching feel. Anything not needed for M2 is out of scope (Part H).

Design pillars (constrain every decision)
The player is the concierge, not the spreadsheet. Staff make the matching game tenser, they don't replace it with a management screen.
Pressure, never stress. An unstaffed station degrades gracefully, never catastrophically. Every squeeze has a release valve. Pause is always available.
Flavor is signal. Every trait is one cost, one benefit, one joke — and the joke teaches the mechanic.
Setbacks are stories. No fail states. The worst outcome of a bad staffing call is a small recoverable dent and a funny moment.
What changes from M1 (the heart of this chunk)
Housekeeping is now people. M1's abstract auto-cleaner is gone. Rooms only get cleaned if at least one staffer is assigned to the Housekeeping station. Clean speed scales with that staffer's Housekeeping skill. Two housekeepers clean two rooms in parallel. Zero housekeepers → rooms just stay dirty (graceful degradation — the day still completes, you just have fewer bookable rooms).
Stations are staffed by allocation, not by "Heads." There is no Head-per-station concept. The player freely assigns any number of staff (including zero, or all four) to any station. Assignment is drag-and-drop, one thumb, sub-second.
Reception staffing slows queue patience decay. Empty reception → patience decays at the base (faster) rate. Staffed → slower, scaling with Reception skill.
Bellhop adds check-in / checkout timing. Bellhops do not perform the check-in decision (the player still drags the guest to the room). Instead, after you seat a guest, a bellhop escorts them up — check-in now takes a few sim-seconds, faster with more/higher-skill bellhops, and grants a small satisfaction boost. With no bellhop, check-in still happens (self-service) but slower and with a small satisfaction nick. At morning checkout, a bellhop assigned to the hotel makes checkouts faster and a little earlier, which flips rooms to dirty sooner and buys more cleaning time before the afternoon rush.
Rush-clean. Drag any staffer onto a specific dirty room to have them clean that room in parallel with the normal housekeeping order, then auto-return to their previous station when the clean finishes. Pulling the Receptionist to rush a clean (leaving reception empty, patience decaying) is the signature move.
Skills + training. Every staffer has a skill level in all four station skills, lopsided by personality. Skills rise passively while working a station and faster via a directed Train assignment. Soft cap.
Traits. 4–5 active traits, each with one mechanical cost, one benefit, and one joke.
You can see and move your people. Staff appear in the hotel as sprites and in a roster/staff menu. Tapping a staffer (in the hotel or the menu) opens a detail card: placeholder portrait, per-station skill bars, level/XP, and traits.
Kitchen station: present but inert

The Kitchen station exists as a valid assignment target and appears in data, but has no effect in M2 (dining is M4). A staffer assigned to Kitchen simply gains Kitchen XP and does nothing else. Do not build any dining/meal logic. (Kitchen is in so the assignment UI and save schema are complete now.)

Part E — Simulation Rules (precise)

Everything below is deterministic and sim-time-based (so 1x and 2x are identical difficulty). "Effectiveness" for a staffer at a station = the level-based multiplier combined with any trait phase multiplier (Deep Sleeper etc.) and Quick Study for XP.

1. Assignment
assign_staff sets a staffer's persistent station. Valid stations: reception, bellhop, housekeeping, kitchen, off_duty. Off Duty = idle, no effect, available to be pulled instantly.
Assigning cancels active training and ends any in-progress rush-clean's auto-return target (if reassigned mid-rush-clean, they finish the current room then honor the new assignment instead of the old one — record this rule in DECISIONS.md).
Any number of staff per station, including zero and all four. No caps.
Emit staff_assigned. If the change drops a phase-relevant station to zero, emit station_understaffed once.
2. Housekeeping = staff-driven cleaning (replaces M1 auto-service)
Dirty rooms form an ordered list in the order they became dirty (unchanged from M1).
Each staffer assigned to Housekeeping cleans one room at a time, taking the next unclaimed dirty room in order. Multiple housekeepers ⇒ multiple rooms cleaned in parallel (one worker per room; a room is never worked by two).
Clean duration for a room worked by staffer S = clean_duration_sim_seconds (existing M1 base) × skill_clean_duration_mult_by_level[S.housekeeping_level-1] × S trait clean mult (Perfectionist 1.25) × S phase effectiveness (Deep Sleeper morning 0.6 → slower, so divide effectiveness in or express consistently — be explicit in code and comment the direction).
Zero housekeepers ⇒ no cleaning happens. Rooms stay dirty. The day still completes normally. This is the graceful-degradation pillar; it is not a bug.
Emit clean_started {room_id, staff_id} / clean_finished {room_id, staff_id}. A room is seatable the instant its clean finishes, including mid-afternoon-queue.
Perfectionist next-stay bonus: when a Perfectionist finishes a room, flag the room; the next stay seated there gets +next_stay_satisfaction_bonus satisfaction, then the flag clears.
3. Rush-clean (the signature move)
rush_clean {staff_id, room_id}: valid only if the room is dirty and the staffer is not mid-escort or training. The staffer leaves their current station (record prior_station), walks to the room (a short travel delay is fine — reuse presentation tween; sim may treat travel as ~0 or a small fixed sim-time, your call, record it), and cleans that specific room in parallel with the normal order — genuinely adding throughput.
Uses the staffer's Housekeeping skill + traits exactly like a normal clean.
On finish: emit rush_clean_finished, then after rush_clean_return_delay_sim_seconds the staffer auto-returns to prior_station (emit staff_assigned back). If the player reassigned them during the rush-clean, honor the newest assignment instead.
The whole point: pulling the Receptionist to rush a clean leaves reception empty (patience decays faster) while a room is turned early. That tradeoff must be felt, not hidden.
4. Reception & patience
Patience base is unchanged from M1 (base_patience_sim_seconds × species.patience_mult).
While ≥1 staffer is assigned to Reception, patience decays at rate × reception_patience_decay_mult_by_level[best_reception_level-1] (use the highest Reception level currently on the desk; multiple receptionists don't stack beyond that — record this in DECISIONS.md).
Empty reception → decay × no_reception_patience_decay_mult (i.e. base, fastest).
Chatterbox aura: if a Chatterbox is assigned to Reception, add reception_patience_aura_bonus as an additional slowdown (further multiply decay down). This is Chatterbox's benefit that offsets its slow-check-in cost.
Soft-slow while press-holding a guest, and hard pause, are unchanged from M1 and apply globally.
5. Check-in = bellhop escort (replaces M1 instant seating)

This is the biggest change to the M1 loop — preserve the feel: the player still drags the guest to the room and that decision resolves instantly from their POV; the escort is a short animation-backed delay, not a second interaction.

On a valid seat_guest: immediately reserve the target room (fit evaluation now treats it as unavailable so it can't be double-booked), remove the guest from the queue, set the party to escorting, and start a check-in task. Emit checkin_started.
With a free bellhop: a bellhop staffer escorts (one party at a time per bellhop; multiple bellhops escort multiple parties in parallel). Duration = checkin_duration_sim_seconds × skill_checkin_duration_mult_by_level[bellhop_level-1] × bellhop trait checkin mult (Brisk 0.8, Chatterbox 1.25) × phase effectiveness. On finish, apply satisfaction boost = checkin_satisfaction_boost_by_bellhop_level[level-1] plus any trait checkin_satisfaction_delta (Brisk −4).
With no free bellhop: the party self-checks-in. Duration = checkin_duration_sim_seconds × checkin_no_bellhop_duration_mult; apply satisfaction nick = −checkin_no_bellhop_satisfaction_nick. (Check-in still completes — graceful degradation.)
If several parties are seated while bellhops are busy, they wait their turn to be escorted (a check-in queue), or self-check-in if no bellhop exists at all. Keep this simple and deterministic; document the ordering rule.
On completion: room flips to occupied, stay clock starts, emit checkin_finished {satisfaction}. Finalize the stay's stored satisfaction here = the M1 seat-time base (base + likes − amber/split/partial penalties, clamped) adjusted by the bellhop boost/nick and any Perfectionist next-stay bonus, re-clamped to [0, satisfaction_cap].
6. Checkout = bellhop-assisted (was instantaneous in M1)
At Morning start, rooms whose stay is complete begin checkout. Checkout now takes checkout_duration_sim_seconds.
If ≥1 bellhop is assigned: checkout duration × checkout_with_bellhop_duration_mult (faster) and checkouts begin checkout_with_bellhop_early_offset_sim_seconds earlier (proactive help). Faster + earlier checkout ⇒ room flips to dirty sooner ⇒ cleaning can start sooner ⇒ more cleaning time before the afternoon rush. This is the reward for keeping a bellhop on.
If no bellhop: checkouts run at full duration, no early offset.
Emit checkout_started, then the existing M1 guest_checked_out when the room actually flips dirty (credit the placeholder cash exactly as M1 did — do not change the economy). Keep magnitudes small so this wrinkle nudges the cleaning race without dominating it.
7. Skills & training (hybrid)
Passive (learning by doing): while a staffer is actively working a station (reception with a queue present, bellhop escorting, housekeeping cleaning — define "working" as actively performing the station's task, not merely assigned-and-idle; record your exact rule in DECISIONS.md), they gain that station's XP at xp_per_sim_second_working × trait xp mult (Quick Study 1.5) per sim-second.
Directed training: train_staff {staff_id, skill} puts the staffer in the training state — off the floor, contributing nothing to any station — gaining the chosen skill's XP at xp_per_sim_second_training (× trait xp mult). Emit training_started. Ends on any assign_staff/rush_clean for that staffer (emit training_ended). No money cost.
Level-ups: XP is cumulative per skill; when it crosses xp_cumulative_thresholds_by_level[n], the level becomes n+1 (1-indexed levels). Emit skill_leveled. Soft cap at skill_max_level (5): XP may keep accruing but level never exceeds 5.
The advertised payoff (vision §4): a well-trained Housekeeper eventually turns every morning checkout before the afternoon rush. That must emerge naturally from Rosa reaching level 5 housekeeping — verify via the pacing test at high skill.
8. Everything else unchanged from M1

Matching/fit evaluation (gold/amber/none), party split & partial seating rules and their satisfaction penalties, pop-ins, arrival generation, the night summary, build menu, save-at-night. Do not alter M1 rules except where Part E explicitly changes them. The fit function still comes from the single authoritative sim function — the UI never computes glow colors itself.

9. Day summary additions

Extend the M1 night summary with a small staff line (plain, one screen): rooms cleaned per staffer, rush-cleans performed, any skill level-ups today, and check-ins handled by bellhop vs self-service. Keep it readable; no new screens.
