# Grand Safari Hotel — A New Manager's Guide

Congratulations, and condolences: you now run a hotel for animals. Pigeons will demand the penthouse. Tortoises will take four days to check out of a two-day stay (don't ask). A capybara will simply get in the pool and stay there. This is your life now.

Here's everything you need to know before your first day begins.

---

## 1. The Big Idea

Guests arrive, you seat them in a room that suits their needs, they pay for their stay, and they leave a review on their way out. Good reviews build your reputation and hand you Hearts (your "quality" currency). Cash keeps the lights on and pays for new rooms. Do this well enough, long enough, and your roadside burrow becomes a legendary resort.

There's no way to lose — cash can go negative and the game will offer you uncomfortable ways to dig out, but it never ends the run. Play it like a puzzle, not a tightrope.

## 2. How a Day Works

Each in-game day runs on a clock, split into four phases:

| Phase | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Morning** | Guests whose stay is over check out. Housekeeping gets to work on their rooms. |
| **Midday** | Today's new arrivals are shown to a room (or turned away, if you can't fit them). |
| **Evening** | Housekeeping finishes up — any room that was checked out of this morning is clean again by tonight. |
| **Night** | The books close: upkeep and wages are paid, reviews and Hearts are tallied, and tomorrow's forecast arrives. |

You control the pace with the buttons in the top-right: **Pause**, **1x**, and **2x**. Pause any time you want to sit and think — every menu in the game pauses the clock automatically while it's open, so you never have to rush a decision.

## 3. Reading the Front Desk

The top bar is your dashboard:

- **Cash** — what you've got to spend. Turns red if you go negative.
- **Hearts** — your quality currency, earned from happy guests. Spent on room upgrades.
- **Reputation** — a 0–100 meter. Rises with good reviews, falls with bad ones and walk-aways. Higher reputation brings more guests through the door.
- **Stars** — your hotel's overall rating. Higher star ratings unlock pricier room types and rarer, more exotic guests.
- **Day** and **Season** — where you are in the calendar.

## 4. The Hotel Floor

The grid in the middle of the screen is your hotel, floor by floor. Every tile is one of:

- **Locked** — greyed out, needs a higher star rating to unlock.
- **Empty** — an unlocked, buildable plot. Tap it to open the **Build** menu.
- **Built and vacant** — ready for a guest.
- **Occupied** — shows who's staying there and whether it's a perfect fit or a mismatch.
- **(cleaning)** — just checked out and being tidied up; it'll be bookable again tomorrow.

Tapping any built room (occupied or not) opens its **Upgrade** menu instead of Build.

## 5. Building Rooms

Tap an empty tile and you'll go straight to a room catalog for that one spot — no need to hunt around the grid first. At the top you'll see:

- **Tomorrow's forecast** — a preview of who's arriving next, generated the night before so you have time to react.
- **Recently turned away** — who you've been disappointing lately, and why. Great hint for what to build next.

Below that, every room type you're currently allowed to build, with its tags, capacity, upkeep, nightly rate, and price tag. Pick one (cash permitting) and it's built on the spot.

## 6. The Room Types

Each room has **tags** describing its environment — this is the whole game of matching guests to rooms.

| Room | Tags | Capacity | Nightly Rate | Build Cost | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cozy Nook | warm, dry, quiet | 2 | 40 | 800 | 1 star |
| Roost Loft | high perch, dry | 4 | 35 | 1,000 | 1 star |
| Lagoon Room | warm, water | 3 | 60 | 1,800 | 1 star |
| Ice Grotto | cold, water | 4 | 120 | 3,000 | 2 star |
| Cavern Suite | dark, quiet, high perch | 3 | 150 | 5,000 | 3 star |
| Tundra Hall | cold, spacious | 2 | 180 | 6,500 | 3 star |

You start with a Cozy Nook, a Roost Loft, and two Lagoon Rooms already built, plus a couple of empty plots ready to go — a decent little starter roster.

## 7. Upgrading a Room

Tap any built room to see its current stats, its purchased upgrades, and what's still available. Upgrades cost **both cash and Hearts**, and do things like add capacity, cut upkeep, boost satisfaction, or even add a new tag to the room (so, say, a noisy Lagoon Room can earn "quiet" and start suiting a whole new kind of guest). The same screen also lets you nudge that room type's nightly price up or down — handy so you don't have to jump menus mid-thought.

## 8. Setting Your Prices

The **Prices** menu lets you set a price multiplier per room type, in small steps. Charge more and every stay in that room type earns more — but each guest has a budget tier (low, mid, or high) and a ceiling for how much of a markup they'll tolerate. Push prices past what a budget tier will bear, and those guests start walking away as "too expensive" instead of booking, even if you have vacancies. Watch your Reports and Reviews to feel out where that line is for each room.

## 9. Who's Knocking? Your Guests

Eight species (so far) come through the door, unlocked in tiers as your star rating climbs. Each one has **needs** (a room must have all of these to be a good fit), **likes** (nice-to-haves that earn bonus satisfaction), a budget tier, typical party size, and typical stay length.

| Guest | Tier | Needs | Likes | Budget | Party Size | Stay |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pigeon | 1 | high perch | — | Low | 1–6 | 1–2 nights |
| Capybara | 1 | warm, water | quiet | Low | 1–3 | 2–4 nights |
| Tortoise | 1 | warm, dry | quiet | Low | 1 | 3–6 nights |
| Penguin | 2 | cold, water | quiet | Mid | 1–4 | 1–3 nights |
| Flamingo | 2 | warm, water | spacious | Mid | 2–6 | 1–2 nights |
| Bat | 3 | dark, high perch | quiet | Mid | 1–8 | 1–2 nights |
| Polar Bear | 3 | cold, spacious | water | High | 1–2 | 2–5 nights |
| Snow Leopard | 4 | cold, high perch, quiet | spacious | High | 1 | 3–7 nights |

A guest seated in a room that covers every one of their needs is a "perfect fit" and reviews well. Seat them somewhere that's missing something, and satisfaction takes a real hit — sometimes still worth it to fill a bed, sometimes not.

## 10. Seating Policy

The **Policy** menu is one big strategic switch:

- **Perfect Fit Only** — a guest is only ever seated in a room that meets every need. If nothing suitable is free, they walk away, even past empty rooms. Fewer guests, but nobody has a bad stay.
- **Fill Vacancies** — if there's no perfect match, guests get squeezed into any big-enough vacant room at a satisfaction penalty. Higher occupancy, but you'll eat some weaker reviews along the way.

Neither is strictly "correct" — it depends on whether you're optimizing for reputation or raw occupancy at any given moment.

## 11. Reviews, Hearts & Reputation

When a guest checks out, their stay's satisfaction score decides three things at once:

- **The review** they leave — positive, neutral, or negative.
- **Hearts earned** — a great stay can earn up to 3 Hearts; a mediocre one earns none.
- **Reputation** — nudges up on a positive review, down on a negative one (and ticks down slightly on every walk-away, too).

Satisfaction is built from: matching their needs and likes, how long they've stayed (longer stays build a little goodwill), and any amenities or upgrades tuned to what they enjoy. It's the one number that quietly drives almost everything else in the game.

## 12. Reports & Reviews

- **Reports** has a Daily Log (every day's cash flow, occupancy, and checkouts) and a Weekly Report (7-day rollups) — the fastest way to see whether a change you made actually helped.
- **Reviews** is the raw feed: every guest's review, in their own words, newest first. Mostly for the flavor — but also a quick gut-check on how you're actually doing.

## 13. Your Team

The **Staff** menu is where you manage the animals actually running the place: a **Receptionist** (check-in speed and quality), a **Bellhop** (arrival service), a **Housekeeper** (keeps rooms turning over quickly and in good condition), and a **Chef** (meal quality — a big lever on satisfaction once dining is part of a guest's stay). Each staff member has their own stats, a personality trait or two (some helpful, some just funny — expect the occasional snack thief), and can be sent for training to level up a stat, at the cost of Hearts and cash and a short "at seminar" absence.

## 14. Hiring

The **Hire** menu shows your current candidate pool, refreshed weekly. You start with a few hand-picked staff with real names and personalities; after that, new candidates are randomly generated, with better talent showing up as your star rating rises. If someone isn't working out — or you just don't like their trait — you can let them go from here too.

## 15. Life in the Lobby

Watch the strip above the hotel grid and you'll see the place actually breathing: guests walking in from the entrance, pausing at reception, and heading up; a bellhop ferrying bags in step with every check-in; and your housekeeper making the rounds, tidying reception between jobs and heading off to freshen up a room the moment a guest checks out. None of this is just decoration for its own sake — it's a live read of what's happening in your hotel without needing to stare at the grid.

## 16. Tips for Your First Few Days

- Build into your empty starting plots early — idle plots earn nothing.
- Check tomorrow's forecast (top of the Build menu) before you commit cash — build for the guests you know are coming, not just the ones you wish would.
- Don't max out prices immediately. Watch the Reports for "too expensive" walk-aways and back off if you see them climbing.
- A few mismatched stays under **Fill Vacancies** won't sink you — but a run of negative reviews will hit your reputation, which quietly throttles how many guests show up at all.
- Save Hearts for upgrades that fix a real gap (a missing tag, a capacity crunch) rather than spending them the moment you have enough.
- If cash ever goes deeply negative, don't panic — the game will offer you a way out. It's meant to sting a little, not end your hotel.

Now go check someone in.
